# The Beast Documentation

**BEAST** is Vernam Lab's shared GPU research computing reservation service. These pages explain who may request resources, how reservations are reviewed, how scheduling works, and the rules governing shared use.

This public guide describes the process without disclosing operational infrastructure details. **Authorized users receive a separate, restricted setup guide after a reservation is confirmed.**

## Why requests and approvals are required

BEAST supports research activities with different computing and security requirements. Allowing every user to allocate resources directly could cause conflicting reservations, incompatible configurations, interruptions to experiments, or damage to shared infrastructure. A recorded request makes responsibilities and resource needs clear, helps administrators coordinate research priorities, and enables approved external collaborators to participate without receiving administrative access. Advisors review each reservation, rather than granting blanket approval for a project, because the workload, duration, resources, and operational risks can differ from one experiment to the next. Routine, advisor-approved requests can be scheduled automatically, minimizing unnecessary administrative work.

Students may submit **multiple future reservations** and advisors may review those requests together in one sitting, while approving or declining each ticket individually. Planning ahead helps students manage time, coordinate experiments, estimate resource needs, and develop practical project-management skills.
